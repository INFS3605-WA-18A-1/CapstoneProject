    Property Management Problem statement (Statement 1)¶

    Property managers at small agencies must coordinate every repair across three parties. They log the tenant's request, get the owner's approval, find a tradesperson who is available, and re-book when access or availability falls through. With tradespeople in short supply, this back-and-forth drags repairs out. Tenants live with defects for longer, and owners pay more when small faults grow into larger damage that insurance may not cover. The coordination load caps how many properties each manager can handle, which limits the agency's ability to grow its rent roll.

    Evidence for Statement 1¶

    # Step in the statement Evidence Source
    1 Repairs involve three parties (tenant, owner or agent, tradesperson) The landlord or agent must arrange repairs. If the tenant can't reach them for an urgent repair, the tenant can arrange it and claim back up to $1,000 within 14 days NSW Government, Getting repairs done
    2 Owner approval is a step Not found in published data Anish Interview
    3 Re-booking and chasing tradespeople takes up property managers' time No survey gives the share of property managers' time spent on maintenance. 66% say their workload is busy or far too busy, and 47% work more than 41 hours a week MRI Voice of the Property Manager 2024 via Real Estate Business
    4 Tradespeople are in short supply All construction trades occupations were in national shortage in 2023, and 50% of technician and trades occupations overall Jobs and Skills Australia
    5 Tenants live with defects for longer 50% of renters lived in homes needing repairs and 10% needed urgent repairs. 70% worried about asking for repairs in case it led to a rent increase or eviction National Shelter / UNSW, Rights at Risk (2025)
    6 Small faults become costly damage "The original issue costs only a few hundred dollars to rectify, while the resulting damage can run into the thousands" EBM RentCover (2026)
    7 Insurance may not cover it Damage from a problem the landlord knew about but didn't fix "may not be covered". Mould is "not usually covered" EBM RentCover (2026), RentCover exclusions
    8 The workload caps how many properties each manager handles The average was 116 properties per property manager in 2023, down from 122 in 2019, so no productivity gain. The report recommends "task specialisation and workflow simplification" Macquarie Bank Real Estate Benchmarking Report 2023 (431 agencies)
    9 This limits agency growth 35% of property managers changed employers in FY2023, and 53% of agencies named retention a top-three concern. 63% of property managers feel overwhelmed, and nearly 2 in 5 are considering leaving or unsure about staying Macquarie 2023, Kolmeo State of Property Management (2026)

    Agriculture Problem Statement: Cattle Stations (Statement 2)¶

    Small family-run cattle stations lose and replace hired staff every year, and each mustering season brings in new jackaroos, jillaroos and backpackers, many with no stock-handling experience. The owner or manager must induct and train each one on the job, covering safety, horses and bikes, stock handling and station routines, while also running the muster. Because this know-how sits with an ageing owner rather than in any shared system, it is re-taught every season and lost when workers leave. New workers get up to speed slowly in the most dangerous industry, where horses, cattle and quad bikes cause many serious injuries, and owners have little time left to adopt labour-saving tools.

    Evidence for Statement 2¶

    # Step in the statement Evidence Source
    1 Stations lose and replace hired staff every year On broadacre farms (beef, sheep and cropping), recruitment "was most commonly due to staff turnover" ABARES, Demand for farm workers (2019)
    1 (continued) Backpackers who do 88 days of farm work in regional Australia qualify for a second-year visa. Farmers "face high turnover of backpackers", which "increases the costs of recruitment" ABARES, Demand for farm workers (2019)
    2 Many arrive without stock-handling experience 40% of broadacre farms that recruited had difficulty doing so. The most common reason was that applicants were "lacking the experience and other required skills for the job". Farms further from population centres had more difficulty ABARES, Demand for farm workers (2019)
    2 (continued) Only about 37% of livestock farm workers hold a Certificate III or higher Jobs and Skills Australia, Livestock Farm Workers
    3 The owner or manager must induct and train each worker The business (a "PCBU" under WHS law) must provide "adequate training, information and instruction to ensure that each worker is safe" Safe Work Australia, Duties of a PCBU
    3 (continued) Small businesses mostly deliver safety training in an "ad hoc, unstructured" way Bluff 2019, Safety Science (46 small and medium businesses)
    4 Training happens while the owner is also running the muster Not found in published data Your interviews
    5 The know-how sits with an ageing owner Livestock farmers have a median age of 62, and 34.6% are 65 or older Jobs and Skills Australia, Livestock Farmers
    5 (continued) Agriculture has the oldest owner-operators of any industry, with a median age of 56 in 2016 ABARES workforce trends
    6 It's re-taught every season and lost when workers leave ABARES notes that keeping workers longer "may increase employers' incentives to provide training", which implies short stays make training less worth the effort. There's no direct measure ABARES, Demand for farm workers (2019), your interviews
    7 The most dangerous industry Agriculture has the highest work fatality rate of any industry, at 13.7 per 100,000 workers in 2024 Safe Work Australia, Key WHS Statistics 2025
    7 Horses, cattle and quad bikes cause many serious injuries Of the serious farm injuries reported in 2024, quad bikes caused 46, horses 15, cattle 12 and motorbikes 11 Farmsafe Safer Farms Report via Farm Weekly (2025)
    7 New workers get up to speed slowly and less safely 30–40% of new employees are injured within their first year Work Healthy Australia
    8 Owners struggle to adopt labour-saving tools Farmers' main barriers to new technology are cost (66%), lack of knowledge (37%) and connectivity (36%). Only 3% of agriculture businesses use AI Roy Morgan 2024, ABS 2024–25
