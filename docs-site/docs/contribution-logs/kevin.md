## Property Management Problem statement

> Property managers at small agencies must coordinate every repair across three parties. They log the tenant's request, get the owner's approval, find a tradesperson who is available, and re-book when access or availability falls through. With tradespeople in short supply, this back-and-forth drags repairs out. Tenants live with defects for longer, and owners pay more when small faults grow into larger damage that insurance may not cover. The coordination load caps how many properties each manager can handle, which limits the agency's ability to grow its rent roll.

## Agriculture Problem statement

> Small horticulture farms take on a fresh intake of seasonal and labour-hire workers every year, many without farm-specific skills. The owner or manager must induct and train each worker on the job, covering safety, tasks and equipment, while also running the harvest. Because this know-how sits with an ageing owner rather than in any shared system, it is re-taught every season and lost when workers leave. New workers get up to speed slowly in the most dangerous industry, crops are lost to labour gaps, and owners have little time left to adopt labour-saving tools.
