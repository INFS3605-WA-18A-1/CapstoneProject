# Problem Statement Refinement

## Problem Statement V 1
**Reframed Problem Statement**
>With aging buildings and lacking incentives to repair, property managers spend too much time coordinating maintainence repairs for severe repairs. It affects the small or individual property managers who spend a majority of their time on communication and matianence admin work. This process bottleneck needs to change because it reduces property managers ability to satisfy residents and gain additional properties for management.

**HMW** help small property managers coordinate severe building repairs with less time spent on communication and admin, so they can keep residents satisfied and take on more properties?

1. Martin, C. et al. (June 2025). [_Rights at Risk: Rising Rents and Repercussions_](https://www.unsw.edu.au/newsroom/news/2025/06/seven-in-ten-renters-scared-to-ask-for-repairs-report). UNSW City Futures Research Centre with the ACOSS-UNSW Sydney Poverty and Inequality Partnership, National Shelter and NARO. It is a national survey of 1,019 private renters.
	- 50% of renters live in homes needing repairs, and 10% need urgent repairs.
	- 74% had some defect or issue. The most common were pests (31%), leaks or flooding (24%), hot water problems (21%) and mould in bathrooms (18%).
	- 68% worry that asking for repairs could trigger a rent increase, and 56% fear eviction. That tension makes repair coordination harder for managers.
	- It is not Sydney-specific, and it covers tenants rather than managers' time.
2. Aidan Devine (Sept 2025). [Dirty truth behind rise of horror living conditions in rental homes](https://www.realestate.com.au/news/dirty-truth-behind-rise-of-horror-living-conditions-in-rental-homes/). Survey of 148 landlords 
	- Finder survey of 148 landlords found that two in five (38 per cent) have had a tenant wait longer than is reasonable for a repair in the last year.

## Team Meeting: 30/09 (Week 3)

**Problem Statement v0.1**
> Property managers spend excessive time chasing and rescheduling contractors for tenant repairs, so repairs drag on. Tenants live with unresolved issues and owners pay more from delays and repeat visits. This admin load caps manager capacity and stops small and mid-sized agencies from taking on more properties.

**Key insights**

- People resist changing how they work, so managing adoption matters as much as the tool.
- If a question only has 1–2 possible answers, the problem statement is too narrow.
- Some trades businesses run without the owner present. How is the owner's knowledge handed over to workers?
- Tenant screening is manual: rental history, bank statements, payslips, ID, tenancy database, pets, cars and a phone interview, plus emailing the previous agent.
- Customer churn is agencies' top concern. AI could help them keep up with legal changes.
- A government-sponsored AI bot carries more trust than a general tool like ChatGPT.
- Advertising, open houses, inspections and travel between properties all take time, and tribunal disputes add prep work.

**5 Whys by issue**

| Issue | Root cause found | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Contractor coordination (maintenance) | Managers rely on small personal tradie lists because there is no trusted, shared contractor directory. Quality is passed by word of mouth, and tradie data (availability, capacity, past jobs) isn't digitised, so AI has nothing to match on. | [PropertyChat](https://www.propertychat.com.au/community/threads/how-do-pms-choose-tradies.84726/), [REIQ](https://www.reiq.com/articles/agency-practice/real-estate-agents-occupiers-liability-and-third-party-entrants), [Climate Control News](https://www.climatecontrolnews.com.au/news/latest/word-of-mouth-still-dominates-the-tradie-economy), [ServiceTitan 2025](https://www.servicetitan.com/blog/australian-tradies-market-report-2025) (43% use basic tools or paper, 70% get work by referral) |
| End-of-lease damage claims | The 14-day tribunal window forces claims to the top of the queue, and most repair and bond claims arrive at lease end. | Team discussion |
| Poor repair quality | Owners push managers to keep repair costs low, so contractors deliver low-quality work. | [Macquarie 2023](https://www.macquarie.com.au/assets/bfs/documents/business-banking/bb-real-estate-industry/macquarie-bank-real-estate-benchmarking-report-2023.pdf) |
| Tenant screening | Vacancy days are a measured KPI but damage isn't traced back to screening, so managers rush slow, error-prone reference checks. | Team discussion |
| Tenant request overload | Requests arrive through many channels and agents manually track, prioritise and follow up with tenants, owners and contractors. | Team discussion |

**Who cares**

- **Actors:** tenant (requests repair), property manager (coordinates), contractor (does the work)
- **Stakeholders:** landlord/owner/strata, prospective tenants
- **Beneficiaries:** contractors billed by the hour

The problem is **complicated**: solving it needs property managers' expertise on practical coordination issues and on what data a solution would need.

**Reframes**

1. *Shift the category:* if contractors fail to meet standard, how might we help property managers verify contractor work quality and expand beyond their personal list?
2. *Zoom out:* if owner buy-in is the blocker, how might we help property managers secure owner approval and budget sign-off through cost optimisation?
3. *Challenge the goal:* if contractors don't want small-agency jobs, how might we make those jobs worth prioritising?

**Success looks like:** managers coordinate maintenance with suitable contractors faster and more easily. Owners are kept updated and spend less on repairs. Tenants are more satisfied and churn less.

**Action items**

- Verify the key issues (maintenance, tenant screening, tribunal disputes) with them.
